Coffee

a short talk

.../demo-unhide.html

The bean

Two species do nearly all the work.

Arabica is sweeter, more acidic, and fussier about where it grows. Robusta has more caffeine, more body, and a good deal more bitterness.

Altitude does most of the rest.

Higher up, the cherry ripens slowly, and the bean ends up denser and more aromatic. This is the whole of what "high grown" means on a bag.

The grind

Grind size is a timer.

Finer grounds give the water more surface to work on, so everything happens faster. Coarser grounds slow it down. That is the only reason espresso and filter want different grinds.

Even is worth more than exact.

A grinder that produces one size extracts evenly. One that produces dust and boulders at the same time gives you a cup that is bitter and sour at once.

The brew

Four things, and only four.

Ratio, grind, temperature, time. Change one and you have changed the cup; change two at once and you will not know which did it.

Start at 1:16.

Sixty grams of coffee to a litre of water is a sane place to begin. Adjust the grind first, and only then the ratio.

Extraction, in numbers

Somewhere between 18% and 22% of the bean will dissolve. Under that and you taste the sour, over it and you taste the ash.

A refractometer will tell you where you are. So, eventually, will your tongue.

The business

Margins are in the milk.

The espresso in a flat white costs a fraction of what the cup sells for. Almost everything you earn per cup, you earn after the shot is pulled.

Waste is the other half.

Beans go stale, milk gets poured away, and the grinder is dialled in on stock you paid for. It is worth measuring all three.

Thanks

Questions, and then the good stuff.

About this demo: “Unhide”

This is the unhide demo: a presentation where two sections are marked to be left out, and a URL parameter reveals the ones it names.

The bar along the top is Simplemenu. Every one of these demos loads it, because it is the quickest way to see which chapters a parameter left standing.

What you have been looking at

The talk above is the short version. One chapter (The business) and one slide inside "The brew" are in the source HTML, but are removed once the presentation starts. Luckily, there is a way to get them back if you request them through a URL parameter.

The markup

data-visibility="hidden" is the mark, and it is Reveal’s own attribute. The two optional sections have it, the ordinary chapters do not, and that is the whole difference between them. The tag beside it is what a parameter says to ask for it.

<section data-name="The brew">
    ...
</section>

<section data-name="The business" data-tag="business" data-visibility="hidden">
    ...
</section>

<section data-name="Thanks">
    ...
</section>

The names are for Simplemenu, which builds the menubar from them. A name is a label and marks nothing, so labelling your chapters does not take them out of the deck.

The config

One line. mode says how the deck behaves, and "unhide" means a parameter can only reveal, never hide.

tagteam: {
    mode: "unhide"
}

So a parameter here only ever reveals. A term that matches nothing changes nothing.

The default is "select", where a parameter picks what is shown and hides every other marked section.

Two separate questions: the markup says what starts out, mode says what a parameter does about it.

Try it

  • ?t=business — reveals the chapter, and its entry in the menubar
  • ?t=nerdy — reveals one slide inside "The brew"
  • ?t=business,nerdy — both
  • ?n=the business — by name, because that chapter has one
  • ?t=typo — a term that matches nothing reveals nothing, and hides nothing
  • demo-unhide.html — back to the short version

A group

A group is a set of tags and/or names under one word, so one short link can ask for several things at once:

tagteam: {
    mode: "unhide",
    groups: {
        full: {
            tags: ["business", "nerdy"]
        }
    }
}

?g=full brings back the chapter and the extra slide together.

Select, the other way round

Unhiding makes a parameter put things back, and only the extras have to be marked.

Selecting is the opposite: the parameter is the presentation, and everything it should be able to hide has to be marked. See the select demo.
